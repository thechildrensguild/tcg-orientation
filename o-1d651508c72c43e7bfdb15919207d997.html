👗 Appearance

You will be representing The Children's Guild from your first day. Please dress in a way that is both professional and comfortable so you can do the work and present yourself well.

If you work directly with children and families, the following are required:

👕 Clothing
  • 👟 Shoes: Closed-toe only — sneakers, flats, or loafers. No sandals, flip-flops, or open-back heels.
  • 👕 Fit: Modest — no exposed midriffs, low-cut tops, sheer fabrics, or short shorts/skirts.
  • 🚫 Graphics: Family-friendly — no profanity, drug or alcohol references, political slogans, or graphics unsuitable for children.
  • 🧢 Head: No hats or head coverings indoors (HR can approve religious or medical exceptions in advance).
💎 Accessories & Personal
  • 💍 Jewelry: Minimal — no long necklaces, dangling earrings, or large rings. They can be grabbed during de-escalation.
  • 💅 Nails: Short enough to make a fist comfortably.
  • 🎨 Tattoos: Cover anything unsuitable for children (profanity, weapons, drug or alcohol references). Other tattoos may remain visible.
  • 🌸 Fragrance: None — no perfume, cologne, or strongly scented products. Many children have sensory sensitivities or allergies.

If you work in an admin role and do not work directly with children and families, please keep things business casual and use professional judgment. The guidelines above are still good to keep in mind — you may be asked to visit a school, take a tour, or join a team meeting on short notice.

Rooms can run cool — bring layers or a jacket.

🛡️ Key Policies — Your Safety and Ours

Your safety is our top priority. The children and families we serve are working through significant emotional, behavioral, and developmental challenges. Children may react in ways that are unpredictable, and our policies exist to protect everyone — you, the children, their families, and your coworkers.

Following these policies is not optional. Please read each one carefully before Day 1, and bring any questions to your supervisor or to Learning & Development.

PCM — Professional Crisis ManagementProfessional Crisis Management (PCM) is a crisis response training that covers safe de-escalation and physical management techniques for situations where behavior escalates to a point of risk. Some examples of these behaviors are biting, hitting, kicking, or running. As part of your orientation, you will become PCM Certified.

Until you complete certification, do not place hands on a child — call a PCM-certified team member for help.
Mandated ReporterAs a TCG team member you are a mandated reporter of suspected child abuse or neglect under Maryland law. If you observe or learn something concerning, report it to your supervisor immediately and follow the formal reporting process. You do not need proof or certainty — reasonable suspicion is the legal standard.

We will train you on what to look for, how to document, and how to file a report during orientation.
Confidentiality (FERPA + Clinical)Everything about children and families — names, addresses, treatment, behavior, education, family situation — is confidential.

Do not discuss children or families outside the work setting, with anyone who does not have a clear need to know, or on any personal device or social media. This applies whether you use first names, initials, or no names at all — identifying details can be pieced together. Confidentiality continues after you leave TCG.
Cell Phone UsePersonal cell phone use is prohibited when you are with children or participating in any training activity — this includes orientation, PCM, CPR, and all required trainings. Phones must be stored away (pocket, bag, locker) and out of sight.

If you must take an urgent personal call, step away to a private space and notify your supervisor first. Repeated personal use during programming is a serious issue.
Photos & Social MediaNever take photos or videos of children on a personal device. Do not post about children, families, coworkers, classrooms, or TCG programs on any personal social media account. This includes photos of children's work that show their names.

Approved photos for marketing or recognition use are taken only by designated staff with written family consent on file.
Professional BoundariesNo personal relationships with current children or their families. Do not share your personal cell number, home address, or social media handles with children or families. Do not accept or give gifts without your supervisor's prior approval.

If a child or family member friends you on social media or contacts you outside of work, decline politely and tell your supervisor right away.
Two-Person / Visibility RuleNever be alone with a child in a closed or private space. If a one-on-one conversation is needed, keep the door open and stay in sight of other staff. This protects you from false allegations and protects the child.

If a child needs private space for emotional reasons, alert another staff member so two adults remain aware of the situation.
Off-the-Clock ContactNo communication with current children or families outside of work hours or outside of approved work channels. This includes texts, calls, social media messages, and in-person interactions outside work-related events.

If you encounter a child or family member in public, a brief greeting is fine — do not extend the interaction or share contact information.
Incident ReportingAny injury, near-miss, restraint, property damage, or unusual occurrence involving children, families, or coworkers must be reported by end of your shift — even small incidents. Your supervisor will walk you through the incident-report form and help you document accurately.

When in doubt, report. We would rather have an unnecessary report than miss a pattern.
Substance UseZero tolerance for alcohol or drugs during your shift. The policy applies on TCG property and any time you are responsible for children, including off-site activities and transportation.

If you are taking a prescribed medication that may affect your ability to perform safely, discuss accommodations with HR before your shift.
Visitors & Sign-InAll visitors must sign in at the front desk and wear a visitor badge. If you see someone unfamiliar in the building without a visitor badge, escort them to the front desk or alert a staff member — do not assume they belong.

This includes family members of children, contractors, and visitors from other TCG sites.
